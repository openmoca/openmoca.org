**For apps people use.** An app that makes the OpenMOCA Commitment promises to
respect how creators want their work shared, shown and used, and people's own
choices. The company behind the app signs
that promise, for a named app and version, and it is listed on openmoca.org.
OpenMOCA does not test it. The signed promise is the company's own, and anyone
can hold the company to it.

**Listings are coming soon.** Until then, no app can show the Commitment mark.

## What it means for you

When an app shows the OpenMOCA Commitment mark, its makers have promised that:

### The creator's wishes are respected

- If a creator says their work may not be used by a certain kind of AI, the
  app does not send it there.
- If a creator's work is never to be used for training AI, it is not.
- If work is rated for older audiences, it is not shown to younger profiles.
- If a creator says their work may not be exported, the app does not let you
  export it.
- The creator's name, licence and copyright stay with their work, and every
  answer shows where it came from.
- The app never makes a creator's wishes looser than the creator set them.

### Your choices are respected

- Your own settings, such as where your reading may be sent or what you want
  to see, are always applied. The app never overrides them with a looser
  setting of its own.
- What you bring into the app or keep private stays yours. It is not shown to
  other people, sent anywhere you have not allowed, or published.
- When something is left out, the app tells you why.

## How it is made

The company publishes a signed statement. It names the company, the app, the
versions it covers, the date, the AI models and services the app uses, the
set-ups it covers, and a contact for challenges. It is listed on openmoca.org.
A new version of the app needs a new statement.

## You can check and challenge it

The promise is signed by the company and listed publicly with the app and
version it covers. If you think an app breaks it, report it to the company and
to [OpenMOCA](/contact/), and the signed promise is your evidence.

## What OpenMOCA does, and does not do

OpenMOCA publishes the rules, lists signed statements, offers the Commitment
mark for listed apps, and removes a listing that is withdrawn or shown to be
untrue. It does not test, audit or guarantee any app.

For software that passes the public tests, there is a different listing:
[OpenMOCA Conformant](/conformant/). The full text of the Commitment, with
the precise list of what a company commits to, is in the
[docs](/project/commitment/).
