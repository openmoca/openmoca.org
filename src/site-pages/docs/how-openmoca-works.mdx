## What is OpenMOCA?

The [Open Knowledge Format](https://github.com/open-knowledge-format/open-knowledge-format)
(OKF) shapes knowledge for AI. **OpenMOCA is for the people who make and own
it.** A MOCA package wraps an OKF bundle in a manifest that carries
declarations: who made the work, who owns it and on what terms it may be used.
The package may be signed. A signature shows who signed, not who owns the
work, and not that what they wrote is true.

**MOCA stands for Modular Ontology, Content & Attestation**: knowledge in
modular packages, organised by an optional ontology, its content kept with the
original sources, and attestations, signatures that show who signed it and
who checked it. Open Readers in TypeScript, .NET and Python read, verify,
search and cite a package the same way, on a server or offline, and every
answer can cite a source you can check.

## The problem

Knowledge that feeds AI is often rebuilt for each model, vendor and pipeline,
and loses its structure and its sources on the way. It is then hard to prove
which version an answer came from, who published it, or whether anyone checked
it. Superseded content can keep being served, because the pipeline is rarely
told it is no longer in force.

## Where it helps

MOCA is not another model, vector store or agent framework. It is the form
your knowledge travels in, so whatever you build answers from the right
version and cites the original source.

## A package, in three layers

A MOCA package wraps an
[Open Knowledge Format](https://github.com/open-knowledge-format/open-knowledge-format)
bundle, a folder of Markdown with YAML frontmatter. The name maps onto
it: the ontology is the structure layer, the content comes with its
evidence, and attestation is the trust that runs through all three, like the
pin in the logo.

## What a Reader gives you

A Reader opens a package, verifies it, and searches it with trust rules
applied: content that is out of force, superseded or outside the caller's
audience never comes back. Every result is a **citation record**: the text,
the package version and digest it came from, a versioned reference to cite,
who signed and reviewed it, and its evidence, marked `matched` when the quote
was found in the original.

When an app hands content to a model, it sends a terms request (where the
model runs, and on what conditions) and the Reader applies the creator's
terms to everything the model receives: records, structure labels and
definitions, text sent for embedding at ingest, and the catalogue entry that
names a package. A package whose terms rule out that model is **closed**: the
model may be told only its title, publisher, whether it is signed and why it
is closed. If the creator set `ai.allowed.mention` to `false`, the package is
**hidden** and the model is told nothing about it
([the terms gate](/spec/reader-contract/#154-the-terms-gate)).

Readers are in-process libraries, one per language, and every one passes the
same [conformance corpus](/spec/conformance/), so a package reads the same way
everywhere.

## Three pillars

| Pillar | What it is | Openness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **MOCA package** | The format: structure, content, evidence, and the identity and trust that cover them | Open standard |
| **MOCA Reader** | The standard way to get anything out of a package: search, structure navigation, citation. In-process, one per language, one conformance corpus | Open source |
| **MOCA application** | The product for one use case, built on a Reader and the agent framework of your choice | Yours |
