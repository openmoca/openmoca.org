<div class="lead">

**You build the apps people use:** a player, a reader, a search, a course or
an AI assistant. OpenMOCA keeps a work together with its sources, its credits
and its creator's wishes, in one sealed file, a MOCA package, so any change
shows. Your app opens it, checks it, searches it and links every quote back to
the original. Every app does it the same way, online or offline.

</div>

## What you get

- **An open specification**, free to use under Apache-2.0
  ([the specification](/spec/)).
- **Libraries:** @@release.readers@@, that open, check, search and quote
  that work. They all give the same results ([get started](/start/)).
- **Public tests:** @@release.suite@@, so anyone can show their software gets
  it right ([the tests](/spec/conformance/)).
- **Tools** to make, check and sign MOCA packages, and a server that connects
  them to AI assistants ([tools](/tools/)).

No permission needed: anyone can build on the standard. Software that passes
the public tests can say it is [OpenMOCA Conformant](/conformant/).

## A package, in three layers

What your app can build on: the work itself to search and quote, how it is
organised to narrow and order results, the original sources to check every
quote against, and the seal and signatures that cover all three.

## Start in three steps

1. **Install a library** for your language: TypeScript, .NET or Python
   ([get started](/start/)).
2. **Open a package, search it and quote from it**, with a link back to the
   original. Before you send anything to AI, check what the creator allows
   ([showing the creator's wishes honestly](/docs/guides/showing-terms/)).
3. **Run the public tests**, or make a package of your own
   ([quickstart](/docs/quickstart/)).

## Beside what you already use

Creators may already tell AI crawlers what they may do, on their own websites:
with the TDM Reservation Protocol, the IETF AI preferences vocabulary or RSL.
Those statements stay on the website. OpenMOCA keeps the same wishes with the
work itself, so they are still there when it is copied. The two fit together:
the same wishes can be stated in both places, and the work's own wishes only
ever make them stricter
([the creator's terms](/docs/guides/creators-terms/#beside-what-you-already-use)).
