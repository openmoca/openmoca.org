## For creators: your work keeps its identity

When your work is copied into an app, important things often stay behind:
your name, the people who helped you make it, its sources, and what you
asked. OpenMOCA packs them in with the work.

- **Your credits, sources and terms stay with the work**, in every copy.
- **It is portable**, not tied to one platform, one AI provider or one app.
- **Any app can read it**, with open Readers anyone can use: a player, a
  reader, a library, a course, or an AI assistant.

Your terms travel as part of the package. Any app can read them, and apps
that make the [OpenMOCA Commitment](/commitment/) promise publicly to honour
them.

## For audiences: know who made it, and back them

The people who listen, read, watch and learn. Whatever app you use, the
creator stays with the work:

- **See who made it**: the creator, the people who helped, and where it was
  first published.
- **Go to the source**: open the original at the passage or the moment, and
  the creator's own site.
- **Respect their wishes**: what the creator ruled out travels with the work.
- **Choose apps that do the same.** An app that makes the OpenMOCA Commitment
  has promised, publicly and in writing, to honour the creator's wishes and
  yours.

[What OpenMOCA gives audiences](/audiences/)

## With or without AI

OpenMOCA works with no AI at all. Any app can read a package: a podcast
player, an e-book reader, a search, a course. AI is why it matters now: apps
answer, summarise and chat using people's work, and the true creator is
getting lost. OpenMOCA keeps the creator with the work, in any app.

## One example: a podcast episode

*An example to show the idea, not a real show.* "Harbour Lights", episode 12,
packaged by its maker. The package carries:

- **Credits.** Published by Example Podcast Ltd. Hosted and written by Ada
  Example. Sam Guest, a guest on this episode, is credited for this episode.
- **Sources.** The episode's audio, described and linked, and its captions,
  carried in the package.
- **The creator's terms.** The creator rules out other companies' AI, machine
  translation, and copying the transcript out of the app. Training is never
  allowed by default.
- **Copyright and a note.** "© 2024-2026 Example Podcast Ltd", and the
  creator's own words: "Listen and ask as much as you like. Please don't copy
  the transcripts elsewhere."
- **What a machine made.** The transcript was made by a speech model, and a
  person checked it.
- **A citation that opens at the moment.** Ask about the harbour lantern, and
  the answer opens the episode at 1:15, where the host says "Every night at
  dusk I light the harbour lantern."

The same works for any digital work: a book's chapters, a course's lessons, a
paper's sections, a guide's pages.

## Any kind of digital work

OpenMOCA is not tied to one industry. A MOCA package can hold:

- podcasts and their episodes
- books and their chapters
- courses and their lessons
- video, from one talk to a series
- research
- guides and documentation

It is not tied to one publisher, AI model or app either.

## Beside what you already use

You may already tell AI crawlers what they may do, on your own website: with
the TDM Reservation Protocol, the IETF AI preferences vocabulary or RSL. Those
statements stay on your site. OpenMOCA keeps your terms **with the work
itself**, so they are still there when the work is copied. The two fit
together: the same terms can be stated in both places
([the creator's terms](/docs/guides/creators-terms/#beside-what-you-already-use)).

## Labels, not locks

OpenMOCA keeps your terms attached to your work, and helps apps respect them.
It is not a lock. An app that ignores the terms can still break them, and
nothing can take back a copy once it is made.

What OpenMOCA does is make the terms visible, so people can check them and
choose apps that respect them. Apps that make the
[OpenMOCA Commitment](/commitment/) promise, publicly, to honour what creators ask
and what people ask, and anyone can hold them to it.

## Short answers

### What is OpenMOCA?

OpenMOCA is an open standard that keeps the creator with their work (their
credits, sources and terms) in any app, with or without AI.

### What is a MOCA package?

A MOCA package holds a piece of digital work with its credits, its sources
and the creator's terms, sealed so that any change shows. It can be signed, to
show who stands behind it. It travels as one `.moca` file.

### How do a creator's terms travel?

Inside the package. They are part of what is signed, so every copy carries
them, and any app that reads the package can read them.

### What is the OpenMOCA Commitment?

A company's signed, public promise that a named app and version honours
creators' terms and people's own choices. OpenMOCA lists it; it does not test
it.

app, a Reader or a tool, with no permission needed. There are
