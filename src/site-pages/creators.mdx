**You make things that matter:** a podcast, a book, a course, a film, a guide,
research, or something else entirely.

When that work is copied, shared or used by an AI app, its identity can get
lost. Your name may disappear. Its sources may be hard to find. What you asked
may not travel with it.

OpenMOCA is an open way to package your work with all of that attached. Your
credits, sources and terms go with it. Any app can read them, and apps that
make the [OpenMOCA Commitment](/commitment/) promise publicly to honour them.

## Not just for AI

OpenMOCA works in ordinary apps too: a podcast player, an e-book reader, a
library, a course. Any of them can read a package and show your name, your
credits and your note beside the work, with no AI at all. AI is why it matters
now: apps answer, summarise and chat using people's work, and the true creator
is getting lost. With or without AI, OpenMOCA keeps you with your work.

## One example: a podcast episode

*An example to show the idea, not a real show.* "Harbour Lights", episode 12,
packaged by its maker. The package carries:

- **Credits.** Published by Example Podcast Ltd. Hosted and written by Ada
  Example. Sam Guest, a guest on this episode, is credited for this episode.
- **Sources.** The episode's audio, described and linked, and its captions,
  carried in the package.
- **The creator's terms.** The creator rules out other companies' AI, machine
  translation, and copying the transcript out of the app. Training is never
  allowed by default.
- **Copyright and a note.** "© 2024-2026 Example Podcast Ltd", and the
  creator's own words: "Listen and ask as much as you like. Please don't copy
  the transcripts elsewhere."
- **What a machine made.** The transcript was made by a speech model, and a
  person checked it.
- **A quote that opens at the moment.** Ask about the harbour lantern, and the
  answer opens the episode at 1:15, where the host says "Every night at dusk I
  light the harbour lantern."

The same works for any digital work: a book's chapters, a course's lessons, a
paper's sections, a guide's pages.

## Keep your credits with your work

A package names the publisher and the people who made the work, with their
roles: author, host, narrator, editor, translator. A part can credit its own
people too, such as the guest on one episode or the translator of one chapter.
When an answer uses that part, the app can name them.

The people who made the work should not become invisible when it is reused.

How: [credits and dates](/docs/guides/credits-and-dates/).

## Keep your sources with your work

A package can carry the original sources, or point to them when you may not
copy them. When someone checks where something came from, the app can open
the original at the passage, the page or the moment in a recording.

How: [protected sources](/docs/guides/protected-sources/), and
[trust and provenance](/docs/trust-and-provenance/).

## Say what you rule out

Your terms are part of the package, sealed in with your work. In this version
of OpenMOCA, terms say what you **rule out**. You can say no to:

- **AI from other companies**: sending your work to a third party's AI model;
- **AI in the app's own service**, or even **on the person's own device**, if
  you want no AI use at all;
- **machine translation**;
- **export**: letting people copy, save or export the work's text out of the
  app. Quoting it in an answer, playing a moment and reading it in the app are
  not export.

**Training is never allowed by default.** Nothing in a package can change
that in this version.

You can also say:

- **how suitable the work is**: whether it is explicit, a rating such as a
  film board's, and the age range it is meant for;
- **who holds the rights**, how to contact them, and whether others may
  repackage the work;
- **the copyright line** to show, and a short note in your own words to show
  beside the work.

Terms can be different for parts of a work: an explicit episode in a clean
show, a photo under someone else's licence, a chapter that stays off other
companies' AI. A part's AI terms can only be stricter than the whole's; its
rating and its licence are its own.

Where you set nothing, the standard default applies. Where the work, a part
of it and the app disagree, the strictest answer wins, and the app can show
which one decided.

How: [the creator's terms](/docs/guides/creators-terms/).

## Keep track of what a machine made

A package can say which parts a machine made, such as a transcript or a
summary, with the name and version of the model, and which of those parts a
person checked. That travels with every quote, so an app can say "made by a
machine, checked by a person". These are your own statements: OpenMOCA does
not detect machine-made content.

Packaging never changes your original files.

How: [machine-made content](/docs/guides/machine-made/).

## Choose how to share

- **Open**, for anyone.
- **Sealed**: encrypted, so only the people you choose can open it.
- **As references**: entries that point to the original, without copying it.

These are different ways to share. None of them makes copying impossible: a
person who can open a package can copy what they read.

How: [protected sources](/docs/guides/protected-sources/) and
[encrypted packages](/docs/guides/encryption/).

## Keep your options open

OpenMOCA is an open standard. Your package is not tied to one AI provider,
app or platform, and anyone can build software that reads it.

## Beside what you already use

You may already tell AI crawlers what they may do, on your own website, with
the TDM Reservation Protocol, the IETF AI preferences vocabulary or RSL. Those
stay on your site. OpenMOCA keeps your terms with the work itself, so they are
still there when it is copied. Podcast feeds carry across too: a feed's
explicit mark becomes the rating, and its "locked" setting says others may not
repackage the show.

## Labels, not locks

OpenMOCA keeps your terms attached and helps apps respect them. It does not
stop an app that ignores them, and it cannot take back a copy once it is made.
It lets people see your terms, check your sources, and choose apps that make
the [OpenMOCA Commitment](/commitment/).
