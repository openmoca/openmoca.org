<div class="lead">

**You make things that matter:** a podcast, a book, a course, a film, a guide,
research, or something else entirely.

When that work is copied, shared or summarised by an app, its identity can get
lost. Your name may disappear. Its sources may be hard to find. How you want it
shared, shown and used, including by AI, may not travel with it.

OpenMOCA keeps all of that with your work, so it goes wherever your work goes.

</div>

## Get the credit when AI answers

More and more, people meet your work through an AI answer, not through your
page. With OpenMOCA, an app that answers from your work can show where every
part of the answer came from: your name, your episode or chapter, the exact
passage or moment, one tap away.

Apps that make the [OpenMOCA Commitment](/commitment/) promise publicly to do
exactly that: to name you, link back to you, and respect how you want your
work used.

And it works without AI too. Any app — a podcast player, an e-reader, a
library — can search your work and quote it, with a link back to the exact
place it came from.

## Keep your credits with your work

OpenMOCA names the publisher and the people who made the work, with their
roles: author, host, narrator, editor, translator. A part can credit its own
people too, such as the guest on one episode or the translator of one chapter.
When an answer uses that part, the app can name them.

How: [credits and dates](/docs/guides/credits-and-dates/).

## Keep your sources with your work

OpenMOCA can carry your original sources with your work, or point to them when
you may not copy them, so every link back has somewhere to go.

How: [protected sources](/docs/guides/protected-sources/), and
[trust and provenance](/docs/trust-and-provenance/).

## Say what you rule out

Your terms are sealed in with your work. In this version of OpenMOCA, terms
say what you **rule out**. You can say no to:

- **AI from other companies**: sending your work to another company's AI;
- **AI in the app itself**, or even **on the person's own device**, if you
  want no AI use at all;
- **machine translation**;
- **export**: letting people copy, save or export the text out of the app.
  Quoting it in an answer, playing a moment and reading it in the app are not
  export.

**Training AI on your work is never allowed by default.** Nothing you set can
change that in this version.

You can also say:

- **how suitable the work is**: whether it is explicit, a rating such as a
  film board's, and the age range it is meant for;
- **who holds the rights**, how to contact them, and whether others may
  repackage the work;
- **the copyright line** to show, and a short note in your own words to show
  beside the work.

Terms can be different for parts of a work: an explicit episode in a clean
show, a photo under someone else's licence, a chapter that stays away from
other companies' AI. A part's AI terms can only be stricter than the whole's;
its rating and its licence are its own.

Where you set nothing, the standard default applies. Where the work, a part of
it and the app disagree, the strictest answer wins, and the app can show which
one decided.

How: [the creator's terms](/docs/guides/creators-terms/).

## Keep track of what a machine made

You can say which parts a machine made, such as a transcript or a summary,
with the name and version of the AI model, and which of those parts a person
checked. That travels with every quote, so an app can say "made by a machine,
checked by a person". These are your own statements: OpenMOCA does not detect
machine-made content.

Your original files are never changed.

How: [machine-made content](/docs/guides/machine-made/).

## Choose how to share

- **Open**, for anyone.
- **Sealed**: encrypted, so only the people you choose can open it.
- **As references**: entries that point to the original, without copying it.

These are different ways to share. None of them makes copying impossible:
anyone who can open your work can copy what they read.

How: [protected sources](/docs/guides/protected-sources/) and
[encryption](/docs/guides/encryption/).

## Keep your options open

OpenMOCA is an open standard. Your work is not tied to one AI company, app or
platform, and anyone can build an app that reads it.

## See it all together: a podcast episode

*An example to show the idea, not a real show.* "Harbour Lights", episode 12,
kept together by its maker with OpenMOCA. It carries:

- **Credits.** Published by Example Podcast Ltd. Hosted and written by Ada
  Example. Sam Guest, a guest on this episode, is credited for this episode.
- **Sources.** The episode's audio, described and linked, and its captions,
  carried with it.
- **The creator's terms.** The creator rules out other companies' AI, machine
  translation, and copying the transcript out of the app. Training is never
  allowed by default.
- **Copyright and a note.** "© 2024-2026 Example Podcast Ltd", and the
  creator's own words: "Listen and ask as much as you like. Please don't copy
  the transcripts elsewhere."
- **What a machine made.** The transcript was made by a speech model, and a
  person checked it.
- **A quote that opens at the moment.** Ask about the harbour lantern, and the
  answer opens the episode at 1:15, where the host says "Every night at dusk I
  light the harbour lantern."

The same works for any digital work: a book's chapters, a course's lessons, a
paper's sections, a guide's pages.

## Beside what you already use

You may already tell AI crawlers what they may do, on your own website, with
the TDM Reservation Protocol, the IETF AI preferences vocabulary or RSL. Those
stay on your site. OpenMOCA keeps your terms with the work itself, so they are
still there when it is copied. Podcast feeds carry across too: a feed's
explicit mark becomes the rating, and its "locked" setting says others may not
repackage the show.

## Labels, not locks

OpenMOCA keeps your terms attached and helps apps respect them. It does not
stop an app that ignores them, and it cannot take back a copy once it is made.
It lets people see your terms, check your sources, and choose apps that
respect them.
