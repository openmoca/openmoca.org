<div class="lead">

**Your organisation runs on what it knows:** policies, procedures, guides,
training and research.

As that knowledge is copied into websites, help centres, chatbots and
artificial intelligence (AI) assistants, it can lose track of who wrote it,
which version it is, and whether it's still current. When an answer is wrong,
it may be hard to see why.

OpenMOCA keeps the author, the source, the version and the rules for its use
with every piece of knowledge, in any app you choose.

</div>

## Answers your people can check

When a chatbot or search tool uses OpenMOCA, every answer shows where it came
from: the document, the version, and the exact passage, one click from the
original. Older versions can be marked as replaced, and policies can carry
the dates they apply, so answers come from what's current
([trust and provenance](/docs/trust-and-provenance/)).

You can seal and sign what you publish, so anyone can check it hasn't changed
and see who signed it. Reviews and sign-offs travel with it
([signing and review](/docs/guides/signing-and-review/)).

And you're not locked in. It's an open standard with open Readers, so you can
change apps, models or suppliers and keep your knowledge, and its history,
intact.

## A package, in three layers

With OpenMOCA, your knowledge travels as a package: one file that holds the
work with everything that belongs with it. What each layer gives your
knowledge: the work itself, how it is organised (the order of a procedure, the
parts of a handbook, what a lesson builds on), the original sources behind it,
and the seal and signatures that cover all three.

## Who it suits

- **Publishers**, whose books, shows and series reach apps they don't run,
  and who want credits, sources and wishes to go with them.
- **Learning providers**, whose courses keep their order and what comes
  first, and whose answers open the lesson at the right moment.
- **Companies with policies, procedures and guides**, who need answers from
  the version in force, internal pages kept internal, and a record of what
  each answer used.

More are in the [use cases](/docs/use-cases/).
