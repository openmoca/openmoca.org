An answer from an AI app can sound convincing. But can you see what it was
based on?

When an app reads from a creator's MOCA package, every answer can point back
to the work it came from. Not just a list of links: the passage, the page or
the moment in a recording that supports it.

## Follow the source

A citation can open the original at the exact passage in a document, or the
moment in a video or a podcast, where the package carries what it needs to
find it. You can check the original for yourself.

![The Try OpenMOCA demo. A search for "how do I publish an agent to Teams" in a video series has found part 14 at 1:50. The video's transcript is open beside the results, with the passage that answers the question highlighted.](../../assets/demo-citation-at-the-moment.webp)

_Shown: a search in [Try OpenMOCA](https://try.openmoca.org), opened at the

A citation shows where an answer came from. It does not prove the source is
right: it lets you check.

## Know who made the work

A package names its publisher and the people who made it, for the whole work
and for its parts: the guest on one episode, the translator of one chapter.
You can see who the work says was involved, and who signed the package.

## See what the creator asked

A package can carry the creator's terms: which kinds of AI may use the work,
whether it may be machine translated or exported, how suitable it is, and who
holds the rights. An app that reads the package can show you them, and keep
them.

A package can also say which parts a machine made, and which a person
checked.

## Choose apps that make the OpenMOCA Commitment

The [OpenMOCA Commitment](/commitment/) is a company's signed, public promise
about a named app and version. It says the app honours what creators ask and
what you ask:

- your own settings are always applied, and never overridden by a looser one
  of the app's;
- what you bring into the app or keep private stays yours;
- when something is left out, the app tells you why.

It is a promise you can read and hold the company to. It is not a test or a
certificate: OpenMOCA lists it, and does not check every action of the app.
If you think an app breaks its promise, report it to the company and to
[OpenMOCA](/contact/).

Listings of apps that make the Commitment come after the 0.7.0 release
([OpenMOCA Conformant and the OpenMOCA Commitment](/marks/)).

## You still have choices

OpenMOCA is an open standard. It is not tied to one app, AI model or
provider. You can look for apps that make the Commitment, read what they
promise, and choose the ones you trust.

If you want to do right by creators: credit them, honour their terms, and
choose apps that do.
